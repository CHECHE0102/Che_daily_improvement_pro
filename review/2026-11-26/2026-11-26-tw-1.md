## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: A research team at National Taiwan University (NTU) has developed two generations of energy-efficient processors designed to run generative AI directly on devices, the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) announced on Wednesday.
P2: Most generative AI services currently depend on remote data centers, which means a phone or car must send data over a network and wait for a response. Processing on the device itself, the NSTC said, allows robots, vehicles, smartphones and factory equipment to react quickly, protect users' privacy and keep working where connections are unreliable.
P3: The project is led by Professor Yang Chia-hsiang of NTU's electrical engineering department, whose guiding principle is to compute only what is necessary and to move as little data as possible. The first chip, built on a 40-nanometer process, identifies the most important relationships in the data before doing any heavy calculation.
P4: According to the council, it was the first reported processor able to speed up both the training and the use of Transformer models, the technology behind tools such as chatbots. The second chip simplifies training further and delivers 1.6 to 7.6 times the energy efficiency of comparable processors reported at the time.
P5: The findings were presented at the IEEE Symposium on VLSI Technology and Circuits, a leading chip conference. A third chip, produced in October on a 16-nanometer process, aims to overcome memory limits.

    zh: 這項研究針對的是一個日益嚴重的瓶頸。

    zh: 如今的生成式AI大多依賴遙遠的資料中心，因此手機和車輛必須傳送資料並等待回覆。

    zh: 其後繼晶片進一步精簡訓練，效率達到當時已發表同類處理器的1.6到7.6倍。

    zh: 第三款晶片於10月以16奈米製程製造，目的在於緩解記憶體頻寬的限制。
