## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: The Extras Department, a Belfast-based agency that supplies extras for film and television across the island, is ending its operations in the Republic of Ireland. It informed members there in an email on Tuesday.
P2: The agency, which has been the only large-scale business of its kind in Northern Ireland since 2007, has more than 20,000 extras, featured extras and stand-ins on its books. Its clients include HBO, Universal, RTÉ and the BBC, and its website had previously advertised the upcoming launch of a Dublin office.
P3: The agency blamed the UK's Right to Work legislation, which has significantly increased its administrative workload. The law requires businesses to verify that a person is legally entitled to work before a job begins, and on 1 October the requirement was extended to contractors and directly engaged workers. Since many extras work for only a single day, compliance has proved difficult.
P4: Members based in the Republic who already hold the right to work in the UK can still accept bookings in Northern Ireland, although travel costs may be high. To remain on the books, they must complete an identity check through an external provider, followed by a video call with the agency.
