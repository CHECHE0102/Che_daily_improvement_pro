## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: Taiwan's banks are increasingly relying on artificial intelligence to spot suspicious activity, uncover weaknesses in their systems and fend off cyberattacks, as threats against the financial sector intensify.
P2: Taiwan Cooperative Bank reported a sharp rise in suspicious external probes and attacks during the first ten months of 2026, with the monthly average roughly twice that of 2025. About 80 percent of the attack volume was concentrated between August and October.
P3: The attacks mainly targeted internet-facing services such as bank websites, online banking and corporate banking platforms. First Bank said its web application firewall blocked around 2.2 million attacks from January to October, noting that probing activity climbed significantly from the third quarter.
P4: In response, First Bank has introduced AI-powered network and endpoint detection, Bank of Taiwan relies on AI-assisted threat detection, and Taiwan Cooperative Bank is trialling AI-based security tools. None of the banks has identified an attack that can be definitively attributed to autonomous AI, although they admit that increasingly automated methods make this harder to judge.
P5: Chen Chia-hung, a consultant at Rays Technology, urged banks to go beyond tools that only detect attacks after the fact. Instead, he said, they should use AI to simulate attacks and probe their own defences, and include AI-driven attacks in routine cybersecurity drills.
