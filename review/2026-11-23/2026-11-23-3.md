## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: Simple changes to the State's main green energy support scheme could lower electricity bills for homes and businesses by reducing the cost of wind power, according to a new industry report.
P2: The study, carried out by consultants Baringa for Wind Energy Ireland, comes as Irish consumers pay the highest electricity prices in the EU. Typical household bills now exceed €1,700 a year, roughly 50 per cent above the European average.
P3: Under the Renewable Electricity Support Scheme, developers bid in auctions and the lowest bids secure a guaranteed price for 15 years. Baringa recommends extending contracts for onshore wind to 20 years and fully linking payments to inflation, rather than the current 30 per cent indexation. Together, the reforms could reduce developers' costs by 23 per cent and cut the price of power from new onshore wind farms by around 11 per cent.
P4: Had the changes applied at the most recent auction, the agreed price could have dropped from €97 to €69 per megawatt hour, the report says.
P5: Wind Energy Ireland chief executive Noel Cunniffe said several measures could be introduced in time for next year's auction, noting that more onshore wind farms are under construction than at any point in the past decade.

   D. The changes would have raised the clearing price.
   explanation: "Had these measures been applied … the clearing price would hypothetically have decreased from €97 to €69 per megawatt hour" — a fall of €28. The word "hypothetically" shows they were not used (A); the actual price was €97 (B), and the price would fall, not rise (D).

   A. To show that the Government wrote the report
   B. To make readers aware of who paid for the analysis and therefore of its possible interest in the findings  <- answer
   C. To prove that the report's figures are wrong
   D. To suggest that consumers asked for the report
   explanation: The analysis was "conducted by the consultancy Baringa on behalf of Wind Energy Ireland" and described as "commissioned by the wind industry", which tells readers the sponsor benefits from the proposals. It was not written by the Government (A) or requested by consumers (D), and the writer does not claim the figures are wrong (C).
