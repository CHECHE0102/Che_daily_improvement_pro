## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: Relations between Ireland and the UK have improved following a landmark agreement between London and Brussels, according to Minister for Foreign Affairs Helen McEntee.
P2: The deal was agreed on Friday by UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham and EU leaders at a summit in Brussels. It is widely seen as the most significant development in trading relations since the 2016 Brexit referendum, and it may mark the beginning of a political thaw.
P3: Its central measure removes the need for certificates and checks on most animal and plant products traded between the EU and the UK from March 2028. The change will also apply to goods moving between Britain and Northern Ireland, while Northern Ireland retains access to the EU single market. The two sides will also reconnect their emissions trading systems.
P4: In addition, a youth experience scheme will allow up to 100,000 young people to spend as long as three years working, studying or travelling in Britain or the EU. The agreement also includes a structured dialogue on migration, a six-month deadline to settle university fees and a commitment to closer defence ties.
P5: UK officials will be consulted on EU rules in areas such as food standards, which Britain will have to follow, but they will have no vote.
P6: In a statement, McEntee described a massive shift in the UK's approach to Europe and said she hoped the reset would bring greater co-operation. Taoiseach Micheál Martin called the removal of agri-food checks a major win for Irish and British firms and suggested that fewer trade barriers would benefit Ireland. Burnham, citing Treasury analysis, said the deal could raise UK economic output by 0.5 per cent.

Q1 [main]: Which statement best captures the main point of the passage?
   A. Ireland has asked the UK to rejoin the EU single market by 2028.
   B. Irish leaders welcome a wide-ranging new UK-EU agreement that eases agri-food trade and is expected to improve relations, although its economic gains are limited.  <- answer
   C. The UK will gain full voting rights on EU food-standards law.
   D. Northern Ireland will lose its access to the EU single market under the new deal.
   explanation: The passage opens by saying relations "have improved substantially as a consequence of a landmark agreement", sets out its provisions such as removing agri-food checks, and ends with Burnham "acknowledging that incremental measures have inherent limitations". UK officials "will not possess voting rights" (C), and Northern Ireland "will nevertheless maintain its access" (D); rejoining the single market (A) is never mentioned.

   D. EU food standards will no longer apply to goods sold in Northern Ireland.
   explanation: British officials "will be consulted on EU legislation in domains such as food standards, with which the UK must comply; however, they will not possess voting rights" — so the UK follows rules it does not vote on. This contradicts (B) and (C), and nothing suggests (D).
