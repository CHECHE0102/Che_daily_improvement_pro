## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: Middle-aged Taiwanese believe the price of a comfortable retirement is rising. A new survey found that people aged 40 to 64 now estimate they will need NT$16.14 million (about US$490,900) to support themselves in old age, up from NT$15.61 million last year.
P2: The figures appear in the 2027 Ageless Society White Paper, published on Tuesday by the DBS Foundation and CommonWealth Magazine. It is based on 1,406 valid responses as well as input from a range of experts.
P3: Respondents said their main financial concerns were medical and long-term care costs, unexpected major expenses, and inflation eating away at their assets. Partly as a result, the proportion planning to prepare through investing climbed from 39.4% to 46.1%, with time deposits and exchange-traded funds gaining popularity.
P4: Even so, 41.2% admitted they lacked confidence in their financial preparations. On health, the share who felt unsure about their condition in later life dropped from 36.5% to 30.7%, yet fewer people reported getting enough sleep, exercising regularly or eating a balanced diet.
P5: Ng Sier Han, general manager of DBS Bank Taiwan, urged people to start early and take advantage of compound interest, even if they can only save small sums. In his view, growing old is not the real problem; failing to prepare is. A Ministry of Labor official said the findings could feed into labor policy discussions.
