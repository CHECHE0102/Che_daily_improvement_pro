## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: Ireland's mental health system will require as many as 641 additional beds over the next 14 years to keep up with rising demand, according to a study by the Economic and Social Research Institute (ESRI).
P2: The research, funded by the Department of Health, projects that capacity in public psychiatric inpatient services may need to increase by up to 37% by 2040, driven mainly by population growth and ageing.
P3: In 2023, the public system had 1,015 acute adult beds for short-term, intensive treatment, a figure that may need to rise by between 107 and 378. Continuing-care beds for people with severe and enduring illness could grow from 517 to about 710, while the Central Mental Hospital may need between 16 and 40 more.
P4: No increase is forecast for child and adolescent inpatient beds, as the teenage population is expected to fall. Even so, the ESRI cautions that long waiting lists in community services make future demand uncertain.
P5: Lead author Dr Aoife Brick stressed that the solution is not simply more beds but the right balance of inpatient and community care. Minister of State Mary Butler said the findings would guide planning as services expand.

   B. An ESRI study projects that Ireland may need up to 641 more mental health beds by 2040, mainly because of population growth and ageing.  <- answer
   C. The Department of Health has already funded 641 new psychiatric beds.
   D. Community mental health services are no longer needed because of extra beds.
   explanation: The passage reports that services "will require up to 641 additional beds over the next 14 years", an increase "attributable primarily to population growth and demographic ageing". The beds are a projection, not already funded (C); child provision is expected not to grow, not to close (A); and the lead author stresses community care too (D).

   D. Community services will be replaced by inpatient beds.
   explanation: The ESRI notes that "substantial waiting lists in community services render future demand inherently uncertain", implying that unmet need could change the real figures. This contradicts (B), does not prove demand will fall (A), and says nothing about replacing community services (D).
