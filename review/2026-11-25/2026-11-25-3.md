## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: The Government is set to close a loophole that has allowed some workers to claim the tax-free lump-sum allowance twice. Under current rules, people can receive up to €200,000 tax-free over their lifetime from redundancy payments and pension savings combined.
P2: If part of that allowance is used on a termination payment, the worker must sign a waiver confirming it has been spent. However, the waiver applies only to the pension linked to that particular job.
P3: This created a gap. Some people transferred their pension into a Personal Retirement Savings Account (PRSA), which was not covered by the waiver, and then claimed a second tax-free lump sum at retirement. The opening has existed since PRSAs were introduced in 2002.
P4: Advisers believe relatively few people benefited. Jim Connolly, head of pensions at Goodbody, said some may have done so unintentionally, while Munro O'Dwyer of PwC said the grey area involves those who deliberately moved funds to exploit it.
P5: The Finance Bill will extend the waiver to PRSAs and other modern pension products, including foreign plans. Subject to the Bill being passed, the change is expected to take effect from 1 January 2027.
