## top-level paragraphs (field "paragraphs", shown when no level is chosen)
P1: A Dublin church has installed two stained-glass windows in memory of people who died from Aids. The windows at St Teresa's Church on Donore Avenue were put in place ahead of World Aids Day on 1 December, during the church's centenary year.
P2: Both windows depict people of various ages and cultural backgrounds, a reminder that the disease does not discriminate. The first features a red Aids ribbon, the red rose of St Teresa and an image of the Holy Spirit.
P3: A rainbow stretches across both windows. The second includes a green carnation honouring Oscar Wilde, the logo of St James's Hospital as a tribute to medical staff, and ivy representing how widely the disease spread. An alphabet along the bottom allows visitors to spell out the name of a loved one.
P4: The design was created by parishioners and members of the parish team, funded by a parishioner, and made by Capital Glass on Clanbrassil Street.
P5: The parish described the installation as a huge honour and said visitors are free to interpret the windows in their own way. The church previously installed two windows commemorating those who died in the Covid pandemic.
